Orthogonal Decomposition

Variance Maximisation & Eigenvector Projection

PCA rotates the 10 audio feature axes so that the new axes (principal components) each point in the direction of decreasing variance. The first component (PC1) captures the most spread; every subsequent component captures the next highest variance while being perpendicular to all previous ones.

Dataset: processed_data/preprocessed_dataset.csv · Sample: 1,500 tracks · Features: 10 continuous audio features (pre-scaled)

How It Works

PCA in Plain English

1. Find the Spread

PCA looks at all 10 audio features together and finds the direction in which the tracks vary the most. That direction becomes PC1.

2. Stay Perpendicular

Each new component captures the next-highest remaining variance while pointing in a completely different direction from the previous ones.

3. Scree Plot Elbow

The elbow is where the variance captured by each new component stops dropping quickly. We keep everything up to that point — {{ results.elbow_k }} components, {{ results.elbow_var_pct }}% of total variance.

4. PC1 & PC2 for Viewing

The 2D chart always shows PC1 on the x-axis and PC2 on the y-axis — the two most informative directions — regardless of how many components are retained.
